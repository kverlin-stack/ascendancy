# World Bible: Ascendancy

The setting, tone and naming for the game. Read this before writing any card names, flavor text, event text or UI copy. Design rules live in `docs/BRIEF.md`; this file covers how the world looks and sounds.

## The premise in one paragraph

In a world much like our early 20th century, a breakthrough in atomic science arrives decades early. The Concord, a battered alliance of free nations, is losing a world war to the Iron Directorate. The Concord's last hope is Project Sunfire, a secret program to harness the new science before the Directorate's armies reach the capital. The weapon ends the war, and its horror convinces the survivors to set down their borders. The nations meet and found the World Assembly, the first government of all humankind. With war behind them, the unified world turns its science outward: first to orbit, then to a habitable world circling a nearby star, and finally to the first human colony beyond Earth.

## Era

The world is our own invention, not an alternate version of real history, so it has no real dates. Its look and technology move with the story:

- **Act I: the war.** Early-to-mid 20th century in feel: telegrams, slide rules, wartime posters, radio broadcasts.
- **Act II: the Ascent.** Picks up years after the Accord, in a 1960s-style space age: chrome, rockets, mission control, optimism.

The jump between the acts is deliberate. It's part of what makes Act II feel like a new game.

## Inspirations (public domain)

We draw on themes and atmosphere from public-domain "scientific romance." **We never copy text, characters or names from these works.** Everything in the game is our own.

| Work | What we take | Where it shows up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H.G. Wells, *The World Set Free* (1914) | The backbone: atomic science discovered early, a war fought with it, and the catastrophe that leads the nations to unite under one world government. Wells's earnest, idealistic voice. | All of Act I and the unification epilogue |
| Jules Verne, *From the Earth to the Moon* (1865) | "Swords into rockets": the war's engineers and arsenals turned to spaceflight. Big ambitions and charming, confident engineering. | Opening of Act II (Orbit stage) |
| Olaf Stapledon, *Last and First Men* (1930) | The long view: humanity as one species with a shared destiny, the scale and awe of moving to a new world. | Act II tone, the Survey and Colony stages, the true ending |

Rules for using the inspirations:

- No quotes or close paraphrases of the source text in game copy.
- No character, place or invention names from the sources.
- Don't draw on later film, TV or comic adaptations of these works. Only the original books are public domain.
- A credits screen will eventually list the books as inspirations. This is good marketing ("inspired by H.G. Wells's forgotten 1914 novel"), not a legal requirement.

## Tone and voice

- **Act I: grim but determined.** Wartime urgency. Short, clipped flavor text, like telegrams and field reports. Hope is scarce and earned.
- **Unification epilogue: quiet, then hopeful.** Acknowledge what the war cost before turning to the future. No triumphalism about the weapon.
- **Act II: wonder and optimism.** Big skies, confident engineering and a species looking up together. Warmer, longer flavor text.
- **Overall voice:** earnest scientific romance. Characters believe in reason and progress. Wit is fine, cynicism is not.

Flavor text examples (style only):

- Research card: *"The numbers refused to agree, so Dr. Vasquez checked them again. And again."*
- War Effort card: *"Hold the river line. Every day there is a day for the laboratories."*
- Event: *"A Directorate courier has been captured with sealed orders. Open them now, or send them to the cipher office and wait?"*
- Act II Mission card: *"For the first time, the whole world watched the same sky."*

## Characters

- All scientists, directors and crew are original characters with invented names from a mix of cultures. Keep it a global cast, since this story ends with all of humanity united.
- Each named character gets a one-line identity (role and personality) to drive card art and flavor text later.
- No real historical people, and no characters based on figures from the inspiration novels.

## Visual direction (for later art work)

- **Act I:** wartime propaganda-poster style. Bold flat shapes, two or three ink colors, screen-print grain.
- **Act II:** 1960s retro-futurist space-age posters. Bright colors, chrome and starfields.
- Prototype art is code-generated vector art in these styles. A commissioned artist may replace it later.
